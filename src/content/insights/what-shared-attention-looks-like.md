Attention is a team resource, not only a personal one. When three people on a team are each interrupted every few minutes, the team as a whole stops thinking together.

## Signs your team's attention has split

* Meetings start with a recap of what people missed while they were elsewhere.
* Messages get read but not answered, because everyone is waiting for someone else.
* People apologize for asking a question that was already answered in another channel.

## What the research suggests

Work on task switching generally finds that returning to a task after an interruption takes time and increases errors. At team level, the cost multiplies, because each person's interruption also pulls on the people waiting for them.

I would caution against reading too much into any single study. The pattern, though, is consistent with what leaders report.

## A simple response: protected hours

Agree on two blocks each week when notifications pause and nobody expects a quick reply. Keep them short at first. Review after a month.

## Take action

Ask your team one question: "When did you last do an hour of uninterrupted thinking at work?" The answers are usually the whole case for change.
