Slow decisions rarely come from lack of intelligence. They come from unclear agreements about how decisions get made.

## The drag is usually hidden

Look at the last decision that took too long. You will likely find one of these:

* Nobody was sure who owned it.
* The information arrived in pieces across several channels.
* Someone revisited it after the meeting ended.

## The one-page protocol

Write these five lines on a single page and agree to them as a team.

1. **Owner.** One named person decides. Others advise.
2. **Input window.** Advisors have a fixed time to respond, then the window closes.
3. **Default.** If no objection arrives, the proposal goes ahead.
4. **Record.** The decision and the reason go in one shared place.
5. **Review date.** Choose when you will check whether it worked.

## Why it works

The protocol removes the need to decide how to decide, every single time. It also treats a reversible decision differently from an irreversible one, which saves your best attention for the choices that matter.

## Take action

Draft the page with your team in a thirty-minute session. Start with just one recurring type of decision, such as budget requests or hiring approvals.
